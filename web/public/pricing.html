Load $5.
Here's what runs.

You load credits; Beckett spends them on your work. One credit is a fixed $0.10 of real underlying spend — the same on every model, so a cheaper model just burns fewer credits for the same task. So the question isn't a rate card, it's simple: load $5, what runs? Every figure below is derived from real runs — — of them — not a spreadsheet someone wished into being.

Where the number comes from

Every credit above is real underlying spend at — each. That spend is four lines, kept apart on purpose — pass-through model cost, compute at a stated hourly rate, one visible platform fee, and human seats billed separately. Add them up yourself; the math re-runs from telemetry every time this page builds.

  • model cost

    Pass-through tokens.

    What the model provider charges, passed straight through at published per-Mtok rates. No markup hidden in the token price. Cache reads bill at a fraction of fresh input — roughly —× — so long-context work stays cheap on the second pass.

  • compute cost

    Wall-clock time.

    Worker wall-clock and browser-session time at a stated hourly rate of — per hour. Today that rate is —. When a task moves onto a VM, this becomes — — a number change, not a new pricing model.

  • platform fee · the margin line

    One number, in the open.

    Our margin, and the only one. A flat — on top of model + compute. It isn't buried in a marked-up token rate or a padded hourly — it's this line, and only this line. If it ever moves, you'll see it move here.

  • human seats

    Per person, per month.

    — per person per month, for the humans in the loop — separate from anything a task costs. Seats pay for presence and access; tasks pay for work. Mixing them is how "per-seat" bills quietly become "per-everything".

05 · the fine print, un-fine

What we don't charge for.

The costs that are ours to eat, not yours to pay. A coworker who bills you for its own mistakes isn't a coworker.

  • Retries we caused. If Beckett crashed, looped, or re-ran itself because of our bug, that compute is on us.
  • Failed review cycles. When our own reviewer sends work back, the rework isn't a line on your bill.
  • Our overhead. Orchestration, scheduling, the plumbing between agents — that's the cost of running the service, not a task cost.
  • Idle presence. A seat sitting quietly in your Discord doesn't meter. You pay when work happens.
